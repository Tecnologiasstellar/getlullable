Sleep stories without ads are narrated audio that will not interrupt itself to sell you
something: no pre-roll before the story starts, no mid-roll arriving while you are already
under, no sponsor read at the exact point where the narrator has finally gone quiet. Most of
what surfaces for that phrase was built for children, but adults ask it for a structural
reason rather than a fussy one. An advertisement is engineered to recapture attention, and a
sleep story only does its job by releasing it. Separating the two means paying for the audio,
downloading it in advance, or both.

## The complaint is not a small one

It gets described as an annoyance, which undersells it. The cost of an ad at 3am is not the
thirty seconds the ad takes. It is the twenty minutes of descent that preceded it, which do
not resume from where they stopped.

Sleep onset is a shallow, reversible slope rather than a door. You can be most of the way
down it and be returned to the top by one sound that is louder, denser and more insistent
than the thing you were listening to — which is a fair description of the average ad break,
because ads are mixed and written precisely to arrest a wandering listener. That is the
[same failure mode podcasts have](/sleep/why-do-podcasts-keep-me-awake/), with the volume
turned up and the timing made worse.

## The research, at its actual size

There is a literature on sound waking people up, and it is worth reporting accurately,
because it is not about phones. In 2018 Basner and McGuire published the systematic review
behind the WHO's European noise guidelines, covering seventy-four studies of environmental
noise and sleep. Their pooled analysis of the polysomnographic work found the odds of an
awakening rising by roughly a third — an odds ratio near 1.35 — for each 10 A-weighted decibel
increase in the loudest indoor level of a noise event, consistently across aircraft, road and
rail. By the GRADE criteria they rated the quality of that evidence moderate.

An update by Smith, Cordoza and Basner in 2022 broadly agreed, and added a detail that cuts
against the tidy version of this story: when survey questions named noise as the source of
the disturbance, the association was far stronger than when they did not. Some of what people
report is attribution — knowing what to blame.

So the honest summary is narrow and dull. This is research on traffic outside houses, not on
an app at arm's length, and it establishes something obvious rather than something dramatic:
during sleep, a louder sound event is more likely to produce an awakening, and the
relationship is graded rather than all-or-nothing. It is enough to say that interruption is a
real mechanism and not a preference. It is not enough to say anything about your particular
2am.

## Sleep stories without music, while we are here

The other half of this search is people who want the story without a bed of ambient sound
under it, and that preference has more standing than it usually gets. In 2021 Riedy, Smith,
Rocha and Basner reviewed thirty-eight studies of continuous white or broadband noise and
sleep. They found heterogeneous methods, results running in both directions, and, by GRADE,
very low quality evidence for continuous noise as a sleep aid — which, as they note, sits
oddly beside how widely it is recommended. They also flag that it may itself affect sleep and hearing.

None of that makes music bad. It makes music optional, and it means a plain voice on silence
is not the austere choice. It is the one with fewer moving parts.

## The trade, stated plainly

Free audio is paid for by someone, and in an ad-supported app the interruption is not a bug
in the product — it is the product's revenue arriving on schedule. There is no version of
that model where the break is guaranteed not to land at the worst moment, because the moment
that pays best and the moment that costs you most are the same moment.

Which leaves two honest options, and they cost something. Pay for the audio, or download it
in advance so that nothing at all is being fetched at [the point in the night where you are
most fragile](/sleep/waking-up-at-3am-mind-racing/). Preferably both — a stream that stalls
is its own interruption.

## Where we come in, honestly

Lullable has no advertising in it, and there is no plausible version of this business where
it could. The whole product is a voice that gets quieter every minute; an ad break is the one
thing it cannot contain. There is a free tier, and a subscription that opens the rest of the
catalogue, and that is the trade being made — you are paying for the absence of something.

If that is not worth money to you, the alternative is genuinely fine: download something long
and dull before bed and play it offline. [What a sleep story is doing](/sleep/what-are-sleep-stories/)
is not proprietary. [Our endings are all given away in advance](/stories/), for the same
reason we do not run ads — nothing in the file should be waiting to grab you.
