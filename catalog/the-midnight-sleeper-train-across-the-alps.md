A little after nine in the evening on a Vienna platform. The commuter crowds went home hours ago and the shops have their shutters half lowered. Along the platform edge, long and dark blue and quiet, is the train, its curtained windows already glowing a soft amber from inside.

The ending, given away now: you sleep through the mountains, the carriages are quietly sorted toward separate mornings somewhere in the small hours, and you wake up somewhere else. You are not required to be awake for any of it — that is the entire point of a sleeper.

## The first minute

> It is a little after nine in the evening, and you are walking down a platform in Vienna. The station is winding down. The commuter crowds went home hours ago. The shops in the concourse have their shutters half lowered. Someone is pushing a slow cleaning machine across the tiles, leaving a damp shining path behind it. Out here on the platform the air is cool. It smells faintly of warm brakes and coffee. And along the platform edge, waiting, is the train. It is long, and dark blue, and quiet…

## Why this one works at night

A sleeper train is the only form of transport whose stated purpose is that you sleep through it, so the story and the vehicle want exactly the same thing from you. It also has a real rhythm built in — rail joints, the sway of a berth, a freight train washing past in the dark — and rhythm you did not have to make yourself is the easiest kind to fall asleep to.
