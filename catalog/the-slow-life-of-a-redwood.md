Late afternoon on the coast of northern California. The light changes first, then the sound — road, wind, gulls, all of it gone within a few steps, as though a door has closed softly behind you. The ground is deep with fallen needles that take your footsteps and give almost nothing back.

The ending, given away now: the summer fog rolls in off the cold Pacific, the forest waters itself in the dark, and the tree does in one night roughly what it did the night before. It has been doing this since before the cathedrals.

## The first minute

> It is late afternoon, and you are walking into a forest on the coast of northern California. The light changes first. It goes green and dim, the way light changes when you step into deep water. The sky is still up there somewhere, but it arrives secondhand, filtered through a ceiling you cannot quite see. The sound changes next. The road, the wind, the gulls. All of it fades within a few steps, as though a door has closed softly behind you. The ground is deep with fallen needles. They are soft and reddish-brown, and they take your footsteps and give almost nothing back. Walking here is nearly silent…

## Why this one works at night

Everything in it happens on a timescale you cannot hurry, and the narration matches. There is no event: the tree does not fall, nothing is threatened, nobody is looking for anything. It is thirty-eight minutes of a quiet place described by someone who is in no rush to leave it.
