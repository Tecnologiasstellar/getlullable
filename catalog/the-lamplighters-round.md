Every evening at dusk, Tobias Marsh leaves his cottage with a brass pole and a ring of keys he no longer needs, and walks the same nine streets in the same order he has walked them since before the war — which war, he declines to specify. There are fifty-one lamps on his round. He knows each one by name, because he named them.

The ending, given away now, per house rules: he lights all fifty-one, and goes home, and has his tea. That is the whole of it. You are free to leave at any point; the round continues without you.

## The first minute

> The evening was the colour of weak tea when Tobias Marsh closed his gate behind him, which was the correct colour for it to be. He counted his steps to the first lamp, not because he needed to, but because the number — forty-one — enjoyed being counted. The first lamp was called Agnes. Agnes lit on the first try, as Agnes always did, being a lamp of sound character, and Tobias said "evening" to her the way you would to a colleague of forty years, which is to say quietly, and without expecting a reply, and he moved along the railings toward the second lamp, which was called Bernard, and which had opinions…

## Why this one works at night

A fixed route, a fixed number, and a man to whom nothing has happened in forty years. The story is a list wearing a coat. Listeners report losing count of the lamps somewhere around the eleventh, which is early, and exactly the point.
