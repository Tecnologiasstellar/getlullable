A Manifesto for Overthinkers

Why your brain was never the problem

Somewhere along the way, you were told you have a sleep problem — and that the cure was to empty your head. A calm voice asked you to breathe into your belly, and your mind, which spends its days holding systems and arguments and forty open tabs, quietly declined.

That was never a failure of discipline. It was a category error. You don't have a broken brain that needs fixing. You have an active one that was left unemployed in the dark — and an unoccupied mind doesn't rest. It goes looking for work. It replays the meeting. It rehearses conversations that will never happen. It runs simulations of Tuesday.

You've tried the alternatives. White noise bored you in the wrong way — your pattern-loving brain found the loop inside five minutes, and then listened for it. Podcasts worked too well; you lay there at 11:45 taking mental notes at 1.5×, wide awake and well informed.

You don't need to force your brain shut. You need to give it something peaceful to ponder.

So we made the opposite of a productivity tool. Lullable reads you true, quietly fascinating things — the patience of Roman concrete, the weather at the bottom of the sea — and slow stories where nothing is at stake, in a warm, unhurried voice that asks nothing back. Endings are given away in the first sentence. Nothing builds toward anything. The volume drifts gently down and never rises again.

And here is the luxury: zero retention required. There is no quiz in the morning. The finest possible outcome is that you learn something brilliant tonight and lose it by midnight, because you drifted into sleep somewhere around the fourth clause. That isn't the content failing. That's the content finishing its work.

Come think about something else for a while.
