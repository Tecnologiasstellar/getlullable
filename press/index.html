The one-liner

40 words. Lift it as written.

40 words

Lullable is a free sleep app that reads fascinating true stories so softly you fall asleep mid-sentence. Every story gives away its ending in the first minute, so there is no reason to stay awake for it. No sleep score. No streaks.

The paragraph

100 words, for a round-up or a listicle entry.

100 words

Most sleep aids fall into two categories: white noise, which is too empty to quiet a busy mind, and podcasts, which are too engaging to fall asleep to. Lullable sits in the gap. It narrates genuinely interesting true stories — ancient worlds, cosmic journeys, gentle nature, cozy tales — in a voice calibrated to lose you halfway through, then fades to silence on its own. The next morning it shows you a receipt: the time you stopped listening and the last line you heard. That is the only metric in the app. There is no sleep score, no grade, no streak to protect.

The hook

The sleep app that refuses to track your sleep. Orthosomnia — anxiety caused by sleep tracking itself — is a documented and growing problem. Lullable is built in deliberate opposition to it: the app measures nothing, grades nothing, and shows you no number in the morning. The only thing it tells you is where in the story you disappeared.

Why it exists

Quotable, on the record.

“I did not need my sleep measured. I needed something to think about that was not my own life. Every sleep app I tried either bored me into staring at the ceiling or interested me into staying awake. So I built the thing in between — and then made sure it told you the ending first, so there was nothing left to stay up for.”

Alberto Villalpando, founder, Lullable
